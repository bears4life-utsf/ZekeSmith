# AI Doesn’t Eliminate Tradeoffs

**Status:** Published  


**Traces to:** Manifesto → Tradeoffs; AI leadership cluster  
**Source:** Copilot research + strengthen interview, October 2026  
**Public rule:** scenes and costs are in. Employee names, accounts, product names, and live deals are out.  
**Naming on the page:** customer ask = more automated estimating; no “interior,” no named rivals, no “industry standard,” no leapfrog plan.  
**Left out on purpose:** Draft 1 checklist; XactGen prove-quality story; security worse-first; concrete leapfrog roadmap; startup generalization; claimed market-share loss.

AI removes some constraints but creates others around quality, verification, trust, and accountability.

---

AI makes building software cheaper. It doesn’t make being wrong cheaper.

A customer came to us and said they needed estimating that was far more automated. Other companies, they said, were already doing it. Those companies were ahead.

I told them I would be surprised if anyone was doing true hands-off automation at the scale that mattered. Most of the industry is working from the same class of underlying models. The demos can look magical. The commercial reality is usually harder.

As we looked into it, we found that some of the solutions being described as automated still relied on people reviewing the work behind the scenes.

That mattered. The market was selling a finished answer. In at least some cases, the operating model was still human review, just less visible.

---

We did not rewrite our roadmap to match the claim.

AI plus a human in the loop is an honest way to ship this work right now. Users often want that. The potential liability makes that review important. We could pursue a more aggressive automation story. Doing that before we can stand behind the results risks the trust and reliability our customers depend on.

What we protected was that trust. What we were willing to sacrifice was the easier story. Some buyers like the flashier offer. Holding the line might cost us some business.

The longer aim is still automation consistent enough that a person only needs a quick review and approval. We are moving that direction. We are not going to pretend we have arrived because someone else’s brochure said so.

We will not put our name on automation we cannot stand behind and expect that name to mean what it meant yesterday.

---

That is the standard I use when people talk about straight-through processing or work that “writes itself.”

We could build a more automated path sooner. It would not always be consistent. Manual estimating is not perfectly accurate either. Accuracy is a little bit of a scale. Consistency is the bar that has to hold. Until automation is near what a careful person would produce, and produces it the same way tomorrow, we keep a human responsible for the result.

Once we explain the risks, customers generally understand why human review still matters.

---

So the tradeoff AI does not eliminate is not only technical. It is also about reputation and accountability. Generation got cheaper. Proof did not. Someone still has to decide whether the output is good enough to trust, who reviews it, and who owns it when it is wrong.

I am not interested in winning a demo race by hiding the human. I am interested in shipping automation we can stand behind.

When the machine can draft the work in minutes, the leadership job is to decide how much verification the name on the product still requires.
